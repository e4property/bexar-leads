# Outreach Scripts — NOF vs APPT

Key difference driving tone: NOF has a real sale date (concrete deadline
to lean on). APPT doesn't yet — the homeowner is often still in denial
(unopened certified mail, avoiding lender calls), so pushing urgency here
tends to backfire. Informational and unhurried outperforms urgent.

## SMS — first contact

**NOF (sale date known):**
> Hi [FIRST NAME], this is [YOUR NAME], a local investor in San Antonio.
> I noticed your property at [ADDRESS] has an upcoming sale date. I buy
> houses directly and can often close in 2-3 weeks — enough time to move
> on your terms instead of the bank's. Worth a quick call? Reply STOP to
> opt out.

**APPT (no sale date yet, softer):**
> Hi [FIRST NAME], this is [YOUR NAME], a local investor in San Antonio.
> I help homeowners who might be dealing with mortgage challenges —
> no pressure, just wanted to see if you'd like to talk through your
> options. Reply "info" if you're open to it, or STOP to opt out.

**Follow-up / check-back (either type, 2-4 weeks after no response):**
> Hi [FIRST NAME], following up in case anything's changed since I last
> reached out about [ADDRESS]. Still happy to talk if it'd help. Reply
> STOP to opt out.

Every text needs the opt-out line — Texas SB140 (2025) extends telephone-
solicitation rules to texts specifically; exposure is $500/violation,
$1,500 if willful, uncapped aggregate. Scrub against the National DNC
list before texting.

## Cold call — opener (first 10-15 seconds is everything)

**Both types, same opener — the pattern-interrupt:**
> "Hi, is this [OWNER NAME]? My name is [YOUR NAME]. I know this might
> come out of nowhere, but I work with homeowners who are going through
> a tough time with their mortgage. I'm not a lender, and I'm not with
> the bank — I buy houses directly. Is that something you'd be open to
> hearing more about? No pressure at all."

**Then branch by type:**

NOF (lean on the known timeline as leverage *for them*, not against):
> "I noticed your property might be heading toward a sale date. I can
> usually close in two to three weeks, which gives you time to move on
> your terms instead of the bank's."

APPT (equity-check framing, no deadline language at all):
> "I work with homeowners who might be behind on payments but still have
> equity in their home. I'd love to talk through your options — there's
> no obligation either way."

## Objection handling

- **"I already have a loan modification in progress"** →
  *"That's great — I hope it works out. Loan mods can take 3-6 months
  though. Would it be okay if I check back in a few weeks, just in
  case?"* (Sets up the follow-up text above.)

- **"Not interested"** →
  *"I completely understand, and I'm not calling to pressure you. Are
  you facing any challenges with the property, or is everything
  handled?"* — gives them a graceful way to correct you if the filing
  was resolved (loan cured, refinanced) rather than assuming disinterest.

- **"How did you get my information?"** →
  *"When a [Notice of Trustee Sale / Appointment of Substitute Trustee]
  is filed, it becomes public record. I reach out to homeowners in that
  situation because sometimes I can help."*

- **"I can't afford to sell / worried about walking away with nothing"** →
  *"That's actually the part I can help with. I cover closing costs, and
  depending on your equity position, you could still walk away with
  cash."*
